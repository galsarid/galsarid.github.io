About

I came to Earth observation through planetary science.

My training is in planetary science, and I spent the first part of my career modeling comets, asteroids, and other small bodies. Since 2019, my day-to-day work has centered on calibration of Earth-observing instruments at NASA Goddard through SSAI. I still work in planetary science, so the two fields now run in parallel.

Background

Education and current work

I received a Ph.D. in Geosciences (Planetary Sciences) from Tel Aviv University in 2010. My doctoral and early postdoctoral work focused on planetary science, especially the thermal, chemical, and dynamical evolution of comets and other icy bodies.

At the University of Hawai‘i and Harvard University, that work expanded into observations, larger numerical simulations, collision and shock problems, and mission-related studies. At the University of Central Florida, I continued my research on cometary, asteroid, and planet-formation science, as well as development of space exploration mission concepts and space-borne instrumentation.

Today I am a Senior Research Scientist at Science Systems and Applications, Inc. (SSAI), working at NASA Goddard Space Flight Center. I'm involved in cailibration and data analysis of the Terra/Aqua MODIS and the NOAA-21/NOAA-20/Suomi-NPP VIIRS instruments, as well as development of planetary science and space exploration concepts.

The subject matter changed more than the working style did: build a physical picture, write down the assumptions, compare it with measurements, and keep revising until the pieces agree.

Missions and mission concepts

Tempel 1 research during my Ph.D.

My Ph.D. work (Tel Aviv U.) included modeling of Comet 9P/Tempel 1 and simulations of the planned Deep Impact experiment, working with Prof. Dina Prialnik, a mission collaborator. This was mission-related research rather than a formal mission-team role.

During my U. of Hawai‘i postdoc appointment, I contributed to development of Proteus, a NASA Discovery mission concept led by Prof. Karen Meech to study main-belt comet 238P/Read and the origin of terrestrial water.
