Earth & planetary science · remote sensing

I work with models and measurements, from comets to Earth-observing satellites.

I’m an Earth and planetary scientist. On the Earth-observation side, my work involves calibration and analysis of satellite data. On the planetary side, my work involves studying comets, asteroids, planet formation, and mission-related science. The common thread is simple: understand the underlying physics, understand the actual measurement, and make sure the two tell a consistent story.

Research

The two sides of my work are closer than they look.

In both fields, I’m trying to infer a physical system from measurements that have limits and uncertainties. Sometimes the system is a comet or asteroid. Sometimes it is the instrument itself. Models tell us what to expect; data tell us where the model needs work.

Planetary science

Small bodies and their evolution

Comets, Centaurs, asteroids, and icy planetesimals: how they formed, how they change, how their orbits evolve, and what present-day activity can tell us about their history.

Earth observation

Instrument calibration

On-orbit calibration and characterization of MODIS reflective solar bands and the VIIRS day–night band, especially long-term changes that matter for the science data record.

Scientific computing

Models and analysis

Numerical models, time-series analysis, and research software in Python, C/C++, Fortran, shell, Linux, and Git.

Earth observation

MODIS after orbital drift

Terra and Aqua are now sampling solar-diffuser geometries outside much of the range characterized early in the missions. My 2022 first-author study examined calibration options for that regime; later team work used additional Aqua yaw maneuvers to characterize the changing geometry.

Why it matters: the goal is to keep reflective-solar-band calibration reliable as these long-running missions move into a new part of their orbital life.

Earth observation

VIIRS day–night-band calibration

I support day–night-band calibration for NOAA-20 and Suomi NPP. The work includes tracking calibration behavior across the DNB’s large dynamic range; collaborative publications also cover NOAA-21 performance.

Why it matters: stable calibration is what makes low-light measurements comparable over time rather than just visually striking.

Planetary science

The Centaur gateway to Jupiter-family comets

Numerical orbital integrations identified a short-lived, low-eccentricity region just outside Jupiter through which many objects pass on their way from the Centaur population to Jupiter-family comet orbits.

Why it matters: the Gateway gives us a specific place to study the dynamical transition and the onset of comet-like physical activity together.

Planetary science

Didymos and ejecta in a binary system

A 2024 study simulated 72 variations of the Didymos system and grouped the resulting ejecta distributions and surface-return times to see which system properties most strongly control the outcome.

Why it matters: the work provides a baseline for predicting how a binary system’s configuration changes where impact ejecta goes and how quickly material returns to a surface.
